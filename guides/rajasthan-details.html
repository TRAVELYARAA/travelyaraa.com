Rajasthan's forts are the reason many travellers visit the state, but they are spread across hundreds of kilometres of desert, hills and farmland. The best fort trip is one where the route, the drive times and the hotels all fit together.

This guide focuses on that planning. It covers the forts that are worth building a trip around, how much time each one needs, sample routes for different trip lengths, and what to look for when booking a stay in each base city, whether that is a heritage haveli inside an old town, a modern hotel with a lift and parking, or a tented camp in the dunes.

  • Best seasonOctober to March. April to June is very hot, and July to September is the monsoon.
  • UNESCO listing"Hill Forts of Rajasthan" (2013): Chittorgarh, Kumbhalgarh, Ranthambore, Gagron, Amber and Jaisalmer.
  • Flying inJaipur, Jodhpur and Udaipur have regular flights. Jaisalmer's flights are largely seasonal.
  • Trip length7 to 10 days covers three or four base cities without rushing.
  • Road daysTry to keep driving to about 5 to 6 hours a day so you still have time to sightsee.
  • Searchable basesJaipur, Jodhpur, Udaipur, Jaisalmer, Sawai Madhopur, Kumbhalgarh and Chittorgarh all returned hotels in our live TravelYaraa test.

The forts at a glance

Six forts are listed together by UNESCO as the "Hill Forts of Rajasthan". Several other forts, such as Mehrangarh in Jodhpur and Junagarh in Bikaner, are not part of that listing but are just as rewarding. Visiting times below are a rough guide for an unhurried visit, not counting travel.

Sample routes

Drive times below are approximate and depend on traffic, stops and the exact road. Check a current map for your dates, and if you are taking trains, check the current timetable.

4 to 5 days: Jaipur and Ranthambore

Spend two or three nights in Jaipur for Amber, Jaigarh, Nahargarh and the old city, then two nights in Sawai Madhopur for Ranthambore Fort and a safari. Sawai Madhopur is a few hours from Jaipur by road and is also on a main railway line.

7 to 8 days: the classic western loop

Jaipur (2 to 3 nights), Jodhpur (2 nights) and Jaisalmer (2 nights). Jaipur to Jodhpur and Jodhpur to Jaisalmer are both long road days of around five hours or more, so leave early and avoid planning a fort visit on arrival afternoons. Outside Jaisalmer's flight season, most travellers return via Jodhpur.

5 to 6 days: Mewar forts from Udaipur

Base yourself in Udaipur for three nights, add one night at Kumbhalgarh to see the walls in the evening and early morning, and visit Chittorgarh either as a long day trip or with one night there. Kumbhalgarh and Chittorgarh each take roughly two to three hours by road from Udaipur.

10 to 12 days: four bases

Jaipur, Jodhpur, Jaisalmer, back to Jodhpur, then Udaipur via Kumbhalgarh, with Chittorgarh at the end if time allows. Breaking the Jodhpur to Udaipur drive at Kumbhalgarh turns a tiring transfer into a fort visit.

Pace yourself

Fort days involve a lot of walking, ramps and steps, often in strong sun. One fort a day, plus an easy evening, is a realistic pace for most travellers.

Where to stay in each base city

Jaipur

  • Old (Pink) City: walk to the City Palace, Hawa Mahal and the bazaars. Lanes are busy, and traffic and noise are part of the experience.
  • C-Scheme and around MI Road: central, with wider roads, modern hotels and restaurants, and a short drive to the old city.
  • Amer and the Amer road: closer to Amber Fort and quieter in the evenings, with fewer options. Searching "AMER, RAJASTHAN" on TravelYaraa returned hotels in our test.
  • Near the station or airport: practical for a one-night stop or an early departure.

In our Jaipur search, the Popular Places filter mainly listed Jaipur itself, so use "Show on map" on each hotel and the "Search by Hotel Name" filter to compare locations.

Jodhpur

  • Old city around the Clock Tower (Ghanta Ghar), below Mehrangarh: rooftop views of the fort and blue houses. Lanes are narrow, and cars may not reach the door.
  • Newer areas such as Ratanada and Residency Road: wider roads, larger hotels and easier parking, a short drive from the fort.

Jaisalmer

  • Inside the fort: unforgettable atmosphere, but lanes are steep and narrow and vehicle access is limited. Jaisalmer Fort is a fragile, lived-in monument, and many travellers choose to stay outside it to reduce the strain on its old buildings.
  • In town outside the fort: easier access by car, often with fort views from the rooftop, and walking distance to the havelis.
  • Desert camps at Sam or Khuri: sunset on the dunes and a night under the stars. In our Jaisalmer search, Popular Places included Sam and Amar Sagar Pol. Check exactly what the plan includes (meals, entertainment, rides), the type of bathroom, and heating or blankets for cold winter nights.

Udaipur

  • Around Lake Pichola (the old city near the City Palace and the ghats): lake views, rooftop restaurants and easy walks to the main sights, but often steps and narrow lanes.
  • Around Fateh Sagar and the newer parts of the city: quieter, often with easier road access and parking.

In our Udaipur search, Popular Places included Pichola, Lake Swaroop Sagar and Central Area, which helps narrow the list. A "lake view" is usually a feature of a specific room, not of every room in the hotel, so check the room name and details.

Smaller fort towns

  • Sawai Madhopur (Ranthambore): many hotels are spread along the road between the town and the park. Check the distance to the park gate or your safari pickup point.
  • Kumbhalgarh and Chittorgarh: far fewer listings than the big cities (37 and 12 hotels respectively in our test search), so book early. If nothing suits you, stay in Udaipur and visit on day trips.
  • Gagron: Jhalawar is the nearest town. Kota, a larger city, returned hotels in our test search and is about two hours away by road.

Hotel counts change with dates and availability. They are given here only to show how much choice differs between a big city and a small fort town.

Heritage haveli, palace hotel, modern hotel or desert camp?

Rajasthan has some of India's most characterful places to stay. Character comes with trade-offs, so match the property to your priorities. The TravelYaraa Property Type filter shows the types in your results, such as Hotel, Resort, GuestHouse, Bed & breakfast, Villa and Hostel.

Read the accessibility lines on each listing carefully. Lines such as "Upper Floors Accessible By Stairs Only" are especially common in older buildings. If you have mobility needs, contact the property before booking.

Fort-visit practicalities

  • Start early. Mornings are cooler and less crowded, and the light is better for photos.
  • Check official timings and tickets. Opening hours, ticket prices and camera charges change, and different authorities manage different forts. Check the official site or ticket counter for each fort. In Jaipur, a composite ticket covers several monuments, including Amber Fort and Nahargarh.
  • Dress for climbing. Expect ramps, uneven stone floors and steps. Wear comfortable shoes and carry water, a hat and sunscreen.
  • Use licensed guides or official audio guides. Ask at the official ticket counter rather than accepting offers outside.
  • Evening shows. Some forts, such as Amber, Kumbhalgarh and Chittorgarh, hold light-and-sound shows. Schedules and languages vary, so check before you plan an overnight stay around one.
  • Ranthambore safaris are booked separately from your hotel through the official Rajasthan Forest Department channels, and safari zones follow a seasonal schedule, with core zones usually closed during the monsoon. Book your safari before your hotel if the safari is the main reason for the trip.

Busy dates. Diwali (October or November), the Pushkar fair (in the Hindu month of Kartik, usually October or November), the Jaisalmer Desert Festival (usually January or February) and the Christmas to New Year period all bring high demand for rooms. Festival dates follow the lunar calendar and change each year, so check them with official Rajasthan Tourism sources.

Flights to Jaisalmer run mainly in the winter season and are limited at other times of year. Check the current schedule before planning a fly-in, fly-out desert trip.

Chain the dates

Make each hotel's check-out date the next hotel's check-in date, and compare the check-out and check-in times shown on each hotel page. After a five-hour drive you may arrive mid-afternoon, which usually suits a standard check-in time, but an early-morning train arrival may not.

Compare the real total, including GST

Heritage and palace rooms in Rajasthan can cross the ₹7,500 per night threshold where GST on hotel rooms rises from 5% to 18% (the slabs in force since 22 September 2025). For example, a room at ₹4,000 per night has ₹200 GST (₹4,200 in total), while a room at ₹12,000 per night has ₹2,160 GST (₹14,160 in total). These examples only show how the slabs work. Arriving in the old cities

  • Drop-off points: in the old parts of Jaisalmer, Jodhpur and Udaipur, cars and taxis may have to stop some distance from the door. Ask the property for the nearest drop-off point and whether someone can help with luggage.
  • Stairs: rooftop rooms and restaurants in havelis are often reached by narrow staircases. Pack a bag you can carry.
  • ID: carry valid government-issued photo ID for the guests, with names matching the booking. Foreign nationals need their passport with a valid Indian visa or OCI card, because properties must record and report foreign guests.
  • Heat and sun: carry water, use sun protection and plan rest in the afternoon from April to June.
  • Temples: remove your shoes where asked, dress modestly, and follow any rules about leather items or photography.
  • Booking details: keep each property's address, phone number and booking details offline, as mobile signal can be weak inside thick-walled buildings and in the desert.

In short

Choose your forts first, then build a route with realistic drive times, and only then pick hotels. In each city, decide between atmosphere (old city, havelis, inside the fort) and convenience (newer areas, lifts, parking). Pick the correct RAJASTHAN entry in the search list, chain your dates, compare the Total Payment including GST, and keep later legs flexible. You will spend your days in the forts, not on the road.
